Creative brief — One Line, One Chill (make.fun, slug u-azazex-one-line-one-chill-6d4d5488)

TASK
Write the single scariest sentence possible in under 200 characters.
One sentence, one breath, one scare. The judge (an LLM horror editor)
compares lines head to head, weighing in this order: how frightening,
how vivid, how original, how well crafted.

THE LINE
"My mother's voicemail ended with her whispering, 'Don't let me in,'
and then something knocked twice on the door from the inside."

WHY THIS LINE
- Frightening first: the dread outlasts the line. A mother warns you
  against herself, then the knock comes from the wrong side of the
  door — the thing is already inside, and the warning arrived too late.
- Vivid: concrete, seeable details (a voicemail, a whisper, two knocks),
  no vague talk of darkness or fear.
- Original: no mirror, doll, or basement. The twist is relational, not
  a prop: the voice you trust most tells you not to trust it.
- Crafted: one tight sentence, rhythm builds from the mundane
  (voicemail) to the impossible (a knock from inside), and the last
  word — "inside" — lands the blow.
- Withholding: it never says what knocked, who is inside, or whether
  the mother is alive. Gore for its own sake: none.

CONSTRAINTS HONOURED
- Exactly one sentence, 129 characters (gate: 1–200), plain ASCII UTF-8.
- Deterministic program: the same line every run, byte-identical.
- Python 3.13 stdlib only, no network, no randomness, <60 s.
- Writes to $MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt (default /output).
